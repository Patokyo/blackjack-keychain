66 × 92 × 19 mm · 200 gsm · no scaling

What to print

This one page, at 100% — actual size, not “fit to page”. 200 gsm card is the sweet spot: stiff enough to hold the folds, thin enough to crease. Anything from 160 to 250 gsm works. Borderless printing is not needed.

Check the scale first

The front panel must measure 66 × 92 mm between its cut lines. Measure before you cut. If it is short or long, the printer scaled the page, and the ends will not close over a deck.

50 mm across — this line must measure 50 mm

Cut and score

Cut along the solid lines; a steel rule and a knife give a straight edge and square corners. Then score every dashed line against the rule with a blunt knife or an empty ballpoint. Score the printed side, and fold each crease away from it, so the ink stays outside.

Glue the body

The four tall panels form a tube. Fold them so the printed faces stay out, then glue the 12 mm tab inside the back panel — the one with the spade on it. Check the tube is square while the glue is still wet; a skewed seam will never close.

Close the ends

  1. Fold in one end: the two small dust flaps first, then the lid, then tuck the 7 mm tongue down inside against the far wall. The tongue has to sit flat, or the lid will not shut square.
  2. Do the bottom first, then drop in the booklet and the board, then close the top over them.

Fill it

Board on the booklet, USB-C end to the top. A deck box is roomier than the board, so slide a cut-down card — or a spare playing card — behind the booklet and the stack sits forward without sliding. The pack stands on its bottom edge, spine out.
