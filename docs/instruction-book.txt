Blackjack keychain - by Patrick Scholefield

PAGE 1
Cover.
Blackjack keychain, with the display and the keyring hole. No badge.
Test your blackjack skills with this compact keychain device.
Hone your basic strategy knowledge in train mode, then compete for a
highscore in play mode.

PAGE 2
Rules of blackjack, written for someone who has never played.
The goal: get closer to 21 than the dealer without going over.
You are dealt two cards, the dealer takes one face up.
Pictures count ten, an ace counts 1 or 11.
Hit for another card, or stand and keep your total. Over 21 is a bust.
You may also double, one card for twice the bet, or split a matching pair
into two hands.
The dealer draws to 17 and stops. Closest to 21 wins and pays 1:1; equal
totals push and the bet comes back.
Two cards making 21 is blackjack, paid 3:2, and beats any other 21.

PAGE 3
The 4 digit display.
Your total on the left pair, the dealer's on the right: 1807 is you on 18,
the dealer on 7. Those digits are the whole hand, so the lights matter as
much as the numbers.
Pair LED, blue: the two cards match, so the hand may be split. 18 with
pair means two 9s.
Soft LED, amber: an ace is counting as 11, and the LED goes out once the
ace must be 1.
A natural 21 shows but is never asked to act.
Letters stand in for words: BET, PLAY, TRN, HIGH.

PAGE 4
Train mode.
Nothing is at stake and every decision is graded against basic strategy.
Right agrees, wrong shows what the book wanted: hit, stand, double or
split. The game records the percentage of correct decisions in each
session, showing it after each hand and when the bank button is held.

PAGE 5
Play mode.
A bankroll to bet from, a bet to set before each hand, and a high score
to chase. The bank is kept between sessions. A short bank goes on the
next hand whole; only an empty bank ends the run.

PAGE 6
Quick button guide.
Hit, stand, double, split, bank, one line each, plus up to four hands,
split aces taking one card, and one button at a time.

PAGE 7
Quick LED guide.
Pair, soft, win, push, lose, right, wrong: what each one means, and the
decisions the game refuses and does not grade.

PAGE 8
Basic strategy, the back page.
The reference sheet, as a grid: your hand down the side, the dealer's
up-card across the top, hit, stand, double or split in every cell. Hard
totals, then soft, then pairs by total.
